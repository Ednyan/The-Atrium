Privacy Policy — The Digital Atrium

Last updated: 2026-10-25. Contact: thedigitalatrium@gmail.com

The Digital Atrium ("the app", "we", "us") is a collaborative canvas application developed by Eduardo Paranhos. This page explains what data the app collects, why, and how you can control or remove it.

Who this applies to

The Digital Atrium is available as a web app and as a desktop app. Some data handling differs between the two — this is noted below wherever it matters.

Account data

You can use much of the app without an account: atriums kept in your browser need none. An account is needed to share atriums, keep them online, and collaborate.

  • An account with an email address needs your email address, a username and a password. Your password never leaves your device: your browser turns it into a key (600,000 rounds of PBKDF2), and only that key is sent. Our server keeps a salted hash of the key, never the password itself. We email a code to confirm your address and to reset a forgotten password.
  • Signing in with Google gives us your name, your email address and your Google account's ID, as Google states them. We never see your Google password.
  • Accounts made before October 2026 were created with Supabase, a backend provider. Until each one is moved over, Supabase still holds its sign-in.

On the desktop app, no account is needed: your username and preferences are stored on your device. You can sign the desktop app in to your account to open atriums shared with you; it then keeps a sign-in for that account.

Content you create

Anything you place on a canvas — text, images, shapes, drawings, embedded links, audio or video — is kept so it can be shown to you and to the people you share the atrium with. Where it's kept depends on the kind of atrium:

  • In your browser: on your device only. It leaves it only if you share the atrium, put it online, or move it to Google Drive.
  • On the desktop app: in the vault folder on your computer, under the same conditions.
  • In your Google Drive: in your own Drive, encrypted in your browser before it's uploaded. The keys are kept in your Drive too, so we cannot read these atriums. The app asks Google only for access to the files it creates and to its own hidden folder in your Drive, never the rest of your Drive.
  • Online: on our servers (Cloudflare), encrypted. If your account keeps its keys with us — an account without Google Drive — this encryption protects the files where they're stored, but not from us; see "Administrator access" below.

A public atrium can be opened by anyone with its address, and its owner can list it publicly. A copy of it is kept on our servers so visitors can open it quickly.

Live collaboration and your IP address

While you're in an atrium with other people, your name, chosen colour, cursor, laser pointer and online status are shared with them in real time, and the changes you make reach them as you make them. This goes through our servers (Cloudflare) and, where possible, directly between your device and theirs. None of it is stored, apart from the changes themselves, which are saved as described above.

A direct connection means the people you connect with can see your IP address, as with any peer-to-peer call. If you'd rather they didn't, turn on Hide My IP Address in your profile: your connections then go through Cloudflare's relay, and the others see the relay's address instead of yours.

Emails

We send emails only for what you do: sign-up and password-reset codes, a welcome when you join and a goodbye when you leave, and messages about your contributions. They're delivered by two email services, Resend and Brevo, which process your email address and the message to deliver it.

The report form sends what you write to our support inbox, the same way. If you're signed in with a confirmed address, that address is included so we can answer you.

Contributions

Contributions are paid through Stripe; we never see your card details. We keep the amount, the currency, the date, Stripe's reference for the payment, and — if you give them — the name you'd like shown on the contributors wall (shown only once approved) and an email address to reach you about your contribution. Records of payments are kept as long as accounting requires.

Cookies and local storage

The Digital Atrium does not use tracking or advertising cookies. Your browser's own storage (not cookies) is used only for what the app needs to work:

  • Keeping you signed in between visits.
  • Atriums kept in your browser, and working copies of the ones you open.
  • Interface preferences — e.g. which atrium you were last in, your chosen colour, language, zoom sensitivity, and similar display settings.

None of this is used to track you across other sites, build an advertising profile, or shared with any third party — so no cookie-consent banner is shown. You can clear this site's storage at any time; it signs you out, resets these preferences, and deletes any atriums kept only in your browser.

Pinterest integration (optional)

If you choose to connect a Pinterest account (Profile Settings → Connect Pinterest), you'll be sent to Pinterest's own sign-in and consent screen. We never see your Pinterest password.

  • We request read-only access to your boards and pins, and your Pinterest username, solely so you can choose a board and import its pins as images onto your own canvas.
  • The access token Pinterest gives us is stored on our servers and is never exposed to other users of the app, including collaborators in a shared atrium.
  • We do not post, modify, or delete anything on your Pinterest account — access is read-only.
  • We do not use your Pinterest data for advertising, and we do not sell or share it with third parties beyond what's needed to make the requested API calls to Pinterest itself.
  • You can disconnect at any time from Profile Settings, which deletes your stored Pinterest tokens immediately. Pins you've already imported remain on your canvas as ordinary content unless you delete them yourself.

Services we use

  • Cloudflare — hosts the website and runs our servers: accounts, online and public atriums, live collaboration, the relay, and file storage.
  • Google — signing in with Google, and Google Drive for atriums you keep there. Only if you use them.
  • Resend and Brevo — deliver the emails described above.
  • Stripe — processes contributions.
  • Pinterest — only for accounts that connect it, as described above.
  • Supabase — the sign-in of accounts made before October 2026, and some image files (atrium previews, drawings), while we finish moving them to Cloudflare.

We do not run any advertising or analytics/tracking scripts on this site.

Data retention and deletion

You can delete individual pieces of content yourself at any time from within the app. You can also delete your whole account yourself, immediately, from Profile Settings → Delete Account. This removes your account, your sign-in, your online atriums and their files, and any connected Pinterest tokens. Atriums in your Google Drive stay in your Drive, as yours; delete them there if you want them gone. The people you shared those atriums with keep the copies already on their own devices. Atriums other people own aren't deleted with your account, and what you placed in them can stay in their copies. This cannot be undone. If you'd rather we do it for you, or you're on desktop, contact thedigitalatrium@gmail.com and we'll process the request promptly.

Security

Access is scoped per account and, for shared atriums, per the roles their owner gives (owner, admin, editor, viewer). Atriums are encrypted where they're kept online or in Google Drive. Your password never reaches us, and Pinterest tokens are never exposed to other users through the app's normal features.

Administrator access

I operate this service, which means I hold the keys to the servers it runs on. In practice that is true of anyone running any hosted app, but I would rather state it plainly than leave you to assume otherwise.

That reaches as far as what's kept on those servers: online atriums, public atriums, and account records. It does not reach atriums kept in your browser, on the desktop app, or in your Google Drive — I cannot open those.

I may look at an online atrium to investigate a bug you report, to look into content reported as abusive or illegal, or to recover data after something goes wrong. When I do, I do so invisibly — I will not appear in the list of people present and my cursor will not be shown — so that looking into a report doesn't announce itself to whoever is being reported.

I don't browse people's private atriums out of curiosity, and I'm not doing anything automated with their contents — no analysis, no training data, no sharing with anyone else. If you would rather your references never sit on someone else's server at all, keep your atriums in your browser, on the desktop app, or in your own Google Drive.

This app is operated by an individual developer, not a company. If you have questions, concerns, or a data request, the fastest way to reach me is thedigitalatrium@gmail.com.

Children's privacy

The Digital Atrium is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from children under that age.
